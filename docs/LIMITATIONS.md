# Limitations

ORBIT is an educational and research-oriented prototype submitted to a hackathon. This document states its limits plainly.

> **ORBIT is not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat, predict disease, or provide validated medical advice. It must not be used for real clinical or mission decisions.**

---

## 1. Synthetic data

- The three crew members are fictional. Names, biographies, roles and avatars are invented.
- Every physiological value, environmental reading, mission event and dose estimate is generated by a deterministic educational model.
- No NASA astronaut medical records, OSDR datasets, clinical datasets or real mission telemetry were used to train, calibrate or validate the model.
- The seven-day history is a seeded warmup designed to give the dashboard realistic-looking context. It is not a record of a real mission.
- Mission SELENE-4 and its lunar outpost are fictional. The epoch date is fictional.

## 2. No clinical validation

- No clinician has reviewed or validated the rules, composite indicators, alert levels or decision options.
- No prospective or retrospective clinical study supports the detection performance.
- Sensitivity, specificity, predictive value and calibration are unknown.
- The alert levels (Normal, Observation, Elevated concern, Requires review) are prototype classifications, not clinical risk categories.
- The app does not triage, diagnose, prescribe or replace a flight surgeon.

## 3. Unvalidated thresholds

- The percentage, standardised-deviation and consecutive-sample rules were chosen so the demonstration produces observable alerts. They are not guidelines.
- The SpO2 review band below 94% is a demonstration flag; it is not a validated clinical threshold and does not establish cabin safety.
- The CO2 elevated and review bands (6,000 and 9,000 ppm) are invented for the scenario. Only the 3 mmHg, 1-hour reference is sourced; the ppm conversion is a proxy.
- Radiation bands (1.2, 2.5 and 5 mSv/24 h; 25 mSv cumulative) are invented review markers, **not** NASA crew exposure limits.
- All thresholds are editable in Settings, which changes the model's behaviour. A judge or user can therefore produce different alerts from the same history.

## 4. Simplified physiological models

- The fatigue, stress, mood, cognitive and reaction-time constructs are linear composites with saturation bounds, not validated instruments.
- Cardiovascular responses are simplified and do not model microgravity adaptation, fluid shifts, baroreflex changes, illness, medications or injury.
- Sleep is estimated from scheduled activity and a modelled quality term, not polysomnography. Sleep stages, circadian phase markers and individual chronotype are not modelled.
- The model does not represent pregnancy, chronic disease, acute illness, trauma or pharmacological treatment.
- Co-occurring changes are reported as context. No causal inference, diagnostic reasoning or differential diagnosis is performed.
- The model uses one fictional habitat and a small event vocabulary. It does not simulate cascading system failures, fire, depressurisation or evacuation.

## 5. Radiation estimate

- Dose is a location multiplier times duration plus a solar particle event factor. It is not a radiation transport calculation.
- Absorbed dose in silicon is not tissue dose equivalent. No quality factors, tissue weighting, geometry or spectrum are modelled.
- Shielding assumptions by module are illustrative. Published lunar and Artemis measurements inform the order of magnitude only.
- The estimate does not predict an individual's health risk, and radiation is deliberately not modelled as an acute cause of vital-sign changes.

## 6. Environmental model

- CO2 is a single well-mixed cabin concentration relaxing toward a target. Real habitats have spatial gradients, sensor error, humidity effects and scrubber dynamics.
- The ppm conversion from 3 mmHg assumes 101.3 kPa and ideal-gas behaviour. The app reports a concentration proxy, not calibrated partial pressure.
- Oxygen and temperature are simple first-order relaxations with a degraded setpoint.
- A cabin reading is not an individual exposure measurement.

## 7. Cognitive assessment

- The reaction-time check runs in a browser using `performance.now()`. Display refresh, input device, operating system scheduling, background load and browser throttling add uncontrolled timing error.
- Five trials are far too few for a reliable estimate. The median is sensitive to attention lapses and false starts.
- The comparison baseline is itself synthetic or derived from a short simulated history.
- The test is not a validated neuropsychological instrument and must not be used to infer impairment, fitness for duty or a diagnosis.
- The self-report instrument (ORBIT Self-Report v1) is invented for this project and is not a validated questionnaire.

## 8. Artificial intelligence

- The cloud assistant is optional and explains a read-only snapshot. It can be wrong, incomplete or misleading even with a constrained prompt.
- It has no access to real medical records and cannot examine a person.
- It cannot override deterministic alerts or modify state, but a user could still misread its prose.
- Local explanations are rule-based templates and are intentionally narrow.
- No AI model is used to decide whether an astronaut is medically safe.

## 9. Data and persistence

- Data is stored in the browser's IndexedDB. Clearing site data, using private browsing, changing browsers or a storage eviction can remove it.
- There is no account, authentication, encryption at rest, audit trail or access control. Anyone with access to the browser profile can read or alter the data.
- There is no server-side patient record and no real synchronization with Earth. The communication-loss feature is a simulation only.
- Export is a JSON download of local state. It is not a signed or regulated medical record.
- Reports are browser-generated snapshots. Printing depends on browser PDF support and colour settings.
- Retention caps intentionally drop older observations (1,200 per crew member) and limit alerts, decisions and reports. A report's charts can therefore be shorter than its original period.
- A corrupted or incompatible stored snapshot is ignored; the app falls back to a fresh seeded mission, which can look like data loss.

## 10. Offline and deployment

- Service-worker installation requires a production build served over HTTPS or localhost. The Vite development server does not register one.
- Offline behaviour depends on the browser and on the service worker having completed installation. A first visit while offline will not work.
- Fonts are requested from Google Fonts; without a network and without a cached copy, the interface falls back to system fonts.
- The optional API is a single Node process with in-memory rate limiting. It is not designed for multi-instance production traffic.
- The server binds to `127.0.0.1` by default and needs a reverse proxy or platform configuration in deployment.
- There is no guarantee that a static host's SPA rewrite is configured correctly; without it, deep links fail.

## 11. Simulation and determinism

- Determinism holds for the same seed, same code version and same sequence of actions. Changing thresholds, advancing different amounts, or interacting in a different order produces different results.
- The browser reaction-time check uses random cue delays, so its results are not deterministic even though the simulation is.
- Time advances are chunked at six hours for detection. A deviation entirely inside a chunk that does not persist to the chunk boundary may be missed.
- The per-rule cooldown of 12 simulated hours can suppress a second, clinically important deterioration.
- Scenario projections are deterministic model branches, not forecasts. They should not be read as expected outcomes.

## 12. Scope excluded from this build

- Authentication, multi-user collaboration, role-based access and audit logging.
- Real sensor integration or wearable data ingestion.
- A clinical decision-support validation programme or regulatory pathway.
- Real Earth synchronization, telemetry uplink or mission-control integration.
- Native mobile applications and assistive-technology certification audits.
- Internationalisation.

## 13. Appropriate use

ORBIT is appropriate for:

- demonstrating a transparent monitoring and decision-support workflow;
- teaching the difference between a measurement, a baseline deviation and a diagnosis;
- discussing explainability, provenance and operational consequences;
- evaluating UI and interaction patterns for spaceflight health software.

ORBIT is not appropriate for:

- diagnosing or treating any person;
- deciding whether an astronaut is fit for duty;
- estimating an individual's radiation risk;
- replacing a flight surgeon, medical officer or clinical protocol;
- making operational decisions in a real mission.
